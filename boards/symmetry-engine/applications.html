Nature

Snowflakes

Every snowflake has six arms because ice crystals are hexagonal. An ideal snowflake has the 12 symmetries of a hexagon: 6 turns and 6 mirrors.

Chemistry

Crystals

Atoms in a crystal repeat in three dimensions. There are exactly 230 ways to do it, the 230 space groups, classified in 1891 by Evgraf Fedorov and Arthur Schoenflies. Like wallpapers, they can only turn by a half, third, quarter or sixth.

Chemistry

Molecules

A benzene molecule has the symmetry of a hexagon. Chemists use a molecule's symmetry group to predict which of its vibrations can absorb infrared light, which is how many substances are identified.

The tilings of the Alhambra in Granada use many of the 17 wallpaper groups, centuries before mathematicians proved there are exactly 17.

Design

Fabrics and floors

Houndstooth cloth is a cm pattern, herringbone is pg, and a brick wall in running bond is cmm. Square tiles are p4m; a honeycomb is p6m.

Engineering

Balance

Space equal blades or weights evenly around a shaft and their pulls cancel exactly, so a fan, wheel or turbine spins without wobbling. Engineers measure how far a real rotor is from that perfect symmetry.

The Measure lab
Physics

Conservation laws

Emmy Noether proved in 1918 that every continuous symmetry of the laws of physics gives a conserved quantity: the same laws tomorrow gives conservation of energy, the same laws everywhere gives momentum, the same in every direction gives angular momentum.
