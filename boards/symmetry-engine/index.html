What is a symmetry?

A symmetry is a move that leaves a shape looking exactly the same. Pick a shape, then turn it or flip it. The faint outline shows where it started. If the shape lands on its own outline, you have found a symmetry. Can you find them all?

Draw with a symmetry

Draw anywhere. The engine copies every stroke with the symmetry you choose: only turns (a pinwheel), or turns and mirrors (a snowflake). Every flower, mandala and rose window is one of these two families.

The rule behind it: with n turns and no mirrors there are n symmetries (the cyclic group Cn). Add mirrors and there are 2n (the dihedral group Dn). Leonardo da Vinci noticed that every pattern around a single point is one of these.

Chapter 3 · free

Seven ways to walk

A frieze repeats along a strip: a border, a railing, a line of footprints. However inventive the design, there are only seven kinds. John Conway named them after the footprints you would leave: hop, step, sidle, jump and their spinning versions. Draw in the strip and watch your mark repeat.

Seventeen wallpapers

Repeat a pattern across a whole wall and the possibilities are still not endless: there are exactly 17 kinds, proved by Evgraf Fedorov in 1891. The Alhambra's tilings use many of them. How symmetric is it?

Real things are almost symmetric: faces, leaves, sensor readings, a spinning machine. The Symmetry Index measures how far something is from perfect balance, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality: it is exactly 1 when every part is equal and falls as the parts drift apart.

Six readings x₁…x₆. Symmetry Index = (x₁ + … + x₆)² ÷ (6 · (x₁² + … + x₆²)). Drag the readings, watch the index, and see which departures matter most.
