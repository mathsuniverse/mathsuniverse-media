WHY THE HOLES STAY EMPTY
Every jump halves the distance to the triangle, so a dot cannot stay away from it. And a dot's last rolls are its address: a jump towards A always lands in A. A hole has no address, so no roll leads there. John Hutchinson proved in 1981 that these three rules leave exactly one shape unchanged; the die only visits it. Its dimension is log 3 / log 2 ≈ 1.585.

FOR STUDENTS AND TEACHERS: five lab experiments
No preparation, 10 to 15 minutes each. The board is free in any browser, and the first one needs only paper and a die.
1. Paper chaos game: draw a big triangle, label the corners 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6, then roll and mark halfway, 40 dots each. Pool the whole class's dots on one sheet. Where does nobody's dot land?
2. Try to break it: start the dot in the middle of the big hole and measure its distance to the triangle after each jump. What happens to the distance?
3. Load the die: on the board, load corner 1 to 90%. Predict first, then run. What changes and what doesn't?
4. Turn the knob: run the triangle at a jump of 1/3, 1/2 and 2/3. Which one makes the three copies just touch? Then try the square and the carpet.
5. Invent a rule: try "never the same corner twice", then place your own corners. Describe what appeared in the comments.
