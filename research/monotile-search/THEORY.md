# Towards the law of aperiodic monotiles (started 9 Nov 2026)

## 1. Exact facts (computed here, sources at the end)

**Hat (Tile(1,sqrt3)), metatiles H, T, P, F.** Supertile contents read from Kaplan's hatviz code (constructMetatiles):
H' = 3H + 1T + 3P + 3F, T' = 1H, P' = 2H + 1P + 2F, F' = 2H + 1P + 3F.
Matrix (rows H,T,P,F; columns H',T',P',F') = [[3,1,2,2],[1,0,0,0],[3,0,1,1],[3,0,2,3]].
Characteristic polynomial (x - 1)(x + 1)(x^2 - 7x + 1), det -1. Area factor per step phi^4 = (7+3sqrt5)/2.
Hats per supertile (metatiles hold 4,1,2,2 hats):
  level 0..4, H-supertiles: 4, 25, 169, 1156, 7921 = 2^2, 5^2, 13^2, 34^2, 89^2 (odd-index Fibonacci squared);
  T-supertiles: 1, 4, 25, 169, 1156 (the same squares, one level behind). Linear scale phi^2 is visible directly.

**Spectre (Tile(1,1)), Spectre and Mystic.** S' = 7S + 1M, M' = 6S + 1M: [[7,6],[1,1]], x^2 - 8x + 1, det 1.
One step scales lengths by sqrt(4+sqrt15) AND reflects; the orientation-preserving double step scales lengths by
lambda = 4+sqrt15, and on H^1 the squared substitution has eigenvalues 4 +- sqrt15 (Baake-Gaehler-Mazac-Sadun).
The Spectre has a self-reflecting substitution, the hat does not.

**Penrose rhombs.** [[2,1],[1,1]], x^2 - 3x + 1, area phi^2, linear phi.

Comparable invariant: mu = linear expansion of the orientation-preserving inflation, s = mu + 1/mu:
| tiling | tiles | mu | s | field of mu | field of tile coordinates | mu in tile field? |
| Penrose | 2 | phi^2 (double step) | 3 | Q(sqrt5) | Q(zeta5) contains sqrt5 | yes (self-similar) |
| Ammann-Beenker | 2 | 1+sqrt2 | (norm -1) | Q(sqrt2) | Q(zeta8) | yes (self-similar) |
| Hat / Tile(a,b), a != b | 1 | phi^2 | 3 | Q(sqrt5) | Q(zeta12) (hat), varies with b/a | no |
| Spectre / Tile(1,1) | 1 | 4+sqrt15 | 8 | Q(sqrt15) | Q(zeta12), real part Q(sqrt3) | no |
| Socolar-Taylor | 1 (non-connected) | 2 | - (not a unit) | Q | Q(zeta12) | limit-periodic, not quasiperiodic |

Key observation: for both connected monotiles the inflation is a unit of norm +1 lying OUTSIDE the field generated by
the tile's own edge directions, so the tiling is not geometrically self-similar with the tile itself (hat -> CAP /
fractiles; Spectre -> deformed model set). Both are cut-and-project with 2-dim internal space, module rank 4 over Z.
Note: mu for the hat does not change along the whole Tile(a,b) family (combinatorial invariant); it jumps from s = 3
to s = 8 exactly at the degenerate point a = b.

## 2. Hypotheses to test (not proved)

H1 (unit law). For a connected monotile whose tilings are quasiperiodic, the orientation-preserving inflation acts on
H^1 by a real quadratic unit mu of norm +1, so s = mu + 1/mu is an integer >= 3.
H2 (outside-field law). mu is not in the real field of the tile's edge directions (Q(sqrt3) for 12 directions,
Q(sqrt2) for 8, Q(sqrt5) for 10). Consequences: a 12-direction monotile cannot have s = 4 (2+sqrt3); an
8-direction monotile cannot have s = 6 (silver^2 = 3+2sqrt2); a 10-direction monotile cannot have s = 3 or 7 (golden).
So a "silver monotile" with silver inflation would be forbidden: an 8-direction monotile needs some other s.
H3 (what fixes s). s is set by the combinatorics of the supertile (number of tiles and how the reflected / special
tile sits in it), not by the edge lengths: constant on Tile(a,b), a != b; changes only at degenerate shapes.

## 3. Tests
T1 Check H2 against every known aperiodic set in the literature (Penrose, Ammann-Beenker, Socolar 12-fold,
   shield, Taylor-Socolar, hat, Spectre, and the newer monotile variants) and tabulate s and the fields.
T2 Read off s from first principles: build the H^1 action for hat and Spectre from the substitution, confirm s = 3, 8.
T3 Use H2 to aim the search: for 8 directions, target outlines whose supertile combinatorics give s outside Q(sqrt2).
