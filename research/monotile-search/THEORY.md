# Towards the law of aperiodic monotiles (started 9 Nov 2026)

## 1. Exact facts (computed here, sources at the end)

**Hat (Tile(1,sqrt3)), metatiles H, T, P, F.** Supertile contents read from Kaplan's hatviz code (constructMetatiles):
H' = 3H + 1T + 3P + 3F, T' = 1H, P' = 2H + 1P + 2F, F' = 2H + 1P + 3F.
Matrix (rows H,T,P,F; columns H',T',P',F') = [[3,1,2,2],[1,0,0,0],[3,0,1,1],[3,0,2,3]].
Characteristic polynomial (x - 1)(x + 1)(x^2 - 7x + 1), det -1. Area factor per step phi^4 = (7+3sqrt5)/2.
Hats per supertile (metatiles hold 4,1,2,2 hats):
  level 0..4, H-supertiles: 4, 25, 169, 1156, 7921 = 2^2, 5^2, 13^2, 34^2, 89^2 (odd-index Fibonacci squared);
  T-supertiles: 1, 4, 25, 169, 1156 (the same squares, one level behind). Linear scale phi^2 is visible directly.

**Spectre (Tile(1,1)), Spectre and Mystic.** S' = 7S + 1M, M' = 6S + 1M: [[7,6],[1,1]], x^2 - 8x + 1, det 1.
One step scales lengths by sqrt(4+sqrt15) AND reflects; the orientation-preserving double step scales lengths by
lambda = 4+sqrt15, and on H^1 the squared substitution has eigenvalues 4 +- sqrt15 (Baake-Gaehler-Mazac-Sadun).
The Spectre has a self-reflecting substitution, the hat does not.

**Penrose rhombs.** [[2,1],[1,1]], x^2 - 3x + 1, area phi^2, linear phi.

Comparable invariant: mu = linear expansion of the orientation-preserving inflation, s = mu + 1/mu:
| tiling | tiles | mu | s | field of mu | field of tile coordinates | mu in tile field? |
| Penrose | 2 | phi^2 (double step) | 3 | Q(sqrt5) | Q(zeta5) contains sqrt5 | yes (self-similar) |
| Ammann-Beenker | 2 | 1+sqrt2 | (norm -1) | Q(sqrt2) | Q(zeta8) | yes (self-similar) |
| Hat / Tile(a,b), a != b | 1 | phi^2 | 3 | Q(sqrt5) | Q(zeta12) (hat), varies with b/a | no |
| Spectre / Tile(1,1) | 1 | 4+sqrt15 | 8 | Q(sqrt15) | Q(zeta12), real part Q(sqrt3) | no |
| Socolar-Taylor | 1 (non-connected) | 2 | - (not a unit) | Q | Q(zeta12) | limit-periodic, not quasiperiodic |

Key observation: for both connected monotiles the inflation is a unit of norm +1 lying OUTSIDE the field generated by
the tile's own edge directions, so the tiling is not geometrically self-similar with the tile itself (hat -> CAP /
fractiles; Spectre -> deformed model set). Both are cut-and-project with 2-dim internal space, module rank 4 over Z.
Note: mu for the hat does not change along the whole Tile(a,b) family (combinatorial invariant); it jumps from s = 3
to s = 8 exactly at the degenerate point a = b.

## 2. Hypotheses to test (not proved)

H1 (unit law). For a connected monotile whose tilings are quasiperiodic, the orientation-preserving inflation acts on
H^1 by a real quadratic unit mu of norm +1, so s = mu + 1/mu is an integer >= 3.
H2 (outside-field law). mu is not in the real field of the tile's edge directions (Q(sqrt3) for 12 directions,
Q(sqrt2) for 8, Q(sqrt5) for 10). Consequences: a 12-direction monotile cannot have s = 4 (2+sqrt3); an
8-direction monotile cannot have s = 6 (silver^2 = 3+2sqrt2); a 10-direction monotile cannot have s = 3 or 7 (golden).
So a "silver monotile" with silver inflation would be forbidden: an 8-direction monotile needs some other s.
H3 (what fixes s). s is set by the combinatorics of the supertile (number of tiles and how the reflected / special
tile sits in it), not by the edge lengths: constant on Tile(a,b), a != b; changes only at degenerate shapes.

## 3. Tests
T1 Check H2 against every known aperiodic set in the literature (Penrose, Ammann-Beenker, Socolar 12-fold,
   shield, Taylor-Socolar, hat, Spectre, and the newer monotile variants) and tabulate s and the fields.
T2 Read off s from first principles: build the H^1 action for hat and Spectre from the substitution, confirm s = 3, 8.
T3 Use H2 to aim the search: for 8 directions, target outlines whose supertile combinatorics give s outside Q(sqrt2).

Sources: Kaplan, hatviz (github.com/isohedral/hatviz); Smith-Myers-Kaplan-Goodman-Strauss, An aperiodic monotile
(arXiv 2303.10798) and A chiral aperiodic monotile; Baake-Gaehler-Mazac-Sadun, Dynamics and topology of the Hat family
(arXiv 2305.05639) and On the long-range order of the Spectre tilings (arXiv 2411.15503).

## 4. A second invariant (Razvan's idea: lambda alone does not decide; another parameter or dimension does)
Three candidates, with what is already known:
- nu, the boundary unit. The Spectre's window (internal-space shape) has a fractal boundary of dimension
  log(5+2sqrt6)/log(4+sqrt15) = 1.11098 (Baake et al.). 5+2sqrt6 is again a unit of norm +1 with nu + 1/nu = 10.
  Boundary length grows by nu per inflation, area by mu^2; dim = log nu / log mu, with mu <= nu < mu^2.
  Hat / CAP: hexagonal (polygonal) window, so nu = mu, dim 1. Penrose: polygonal, nu = mu.
  So the pair (s, s') = (mu + 1/mu, nu + 1/nu): Penrose (3,3), hat (3,3), Spectre (8,10). Spectre is the only one
  with s' != s. Conjecture H4: a monotile is classified by (s, s'), and a predicted s is realised only with
  the right s'.
- The ideal class of the return module. Spectre: return module is a NON-principal ideal in Z[xi, 4+sqrt15], field
  Q(sqrt-3, sqrt-5) of class number 2. Hat: field Q(sqrt-3, sqrt5) (to be checked: class number 1?). A second,
  discrete invariant that lambda does not see.
- One more internal dimension. Both known monotiles have module rank 4 (internal space dim 2) and quadratic mu.
  If the quadratic mu predicted for a case is impossible (e.g. silver for 8 directions, by H2), the tile may need
  a cubic Pisot unit and a rank-6 module (internal dim 4). This is the "extra dimension" route.

## 5. Test of H4 (9 Nov) - with a correction
Correction: the hat's window is NOT polygonal. Baake et al. (Diffraction of the Hat and Spectre tilings, arXiv
2502.03268): the CAP/hat window is a hexagon whose subwindows have fractal boundary parts of dimension
log(2+sqrt3) / (2 log phi) = 1.36838. So nu_hat = 2+sqrt3, s'_hat = 4.

| tiling | tiles | mu (linear, orientation-preserving) | s | nu (boundary) | s' | boundary dim |
| Penrose | 2 | phi^2 (2 steps) | 3 | = mu (polygonal) | 3 | 1 |
| Ammann-Beenker | 2 | (1+sqrt2)^2 (2 steps) | 6 | = mu (polygonal) | 6 | 1 |
| Hat (CAP) | 1 | phi^2 | 3 | 2+sqrt3 | 4 | 1.36838 |
| Spectre (CASPr) | 1 | 4+sqrt15 | 8 | 5+2sqrt6 | 10 | 1.11098 |

Observations (verified numerically):
- In both monotiles s' != s: the window is fractal. In the polygonal-window (2-tile) cases s' = s.
- Every unit is the square of a sum of two square roots, and sqrt3 appears in all four monotile units:
  hat:     mu = ((1+sqrt5)/2)^2,        nu = ((1+sqrt3)/sqrt2)^2
  Spectre: mu = ((sqrt3+sqrt5)/sqrt2)^2, nu = (sqrt2+sqrt3)^2
- nu_hat = 2+sqrt3 lies in the tile's own field Q(sqrt3); nu_Spectre does not (sqrt6).
- Caution: nu need not be quadratic in general (a 1D silver-mean variant has a cubic boundary unit, root of
  x^3 - 2x^2 - 1), so (s, s') is special to these cases, not a general fact.
Status: H4 consistent with all available data, but only two monotiles have known windows - too few to call it a law.
Revised conjecture H5: an aperiodic monotile (connected, quasiperiodic) has a fractal window, s' != s.

## 6. Windows computed from scratch (9 Nov), code in window/
Method: lift every tile to the 4D integer lattice (hat: generic Tile(a,b) lift, a-edges and b-edges counted
separately, 0 inconsistencies over 2.26M vertices; Spectre: exact arithmetic in Z[zeta12]); fit the inflation as a
4x4 matrix between consecutive levels; the hidden space is its contracting eigenspace; the windows are the attractor
of the resulting graph-directed map system (hat 24 classes / 132 maps, Spectre 54 classes / 3354 maps).

Exact inflation matrices on the 4D lattice (fit residual ~1e-12, integer):
  hat (one step):       [[2,1,-2,-2],[-1,1,2,0],[0,1,1,-1],[-1,-1,1,2]]   char. poly (x^2 - 3x + 1)^2, eigenvalues phi^{+-2}
  Spectre (double step): [[4,4,-3,5],[1,4,-4,3],[3,-5,7,-1],[4,-3,5,1]]    char. poly (x^2 - 8x + 1)^2, eigenvalues 4 +- sqrt15
So s = 3 and s = 8 are read off directly (trace = 2s).

Window boundary growth per inflation step (boxes at matched scales):
  hat: F-windows exactly phi^2 = 2.618 (polygonal, dim 1); H, P, T windows: total ratio rising 3.3 -> 3.54,
       fractal part (straight part removed) 3.75-3.8 -> consistent with 2+sqrt3 = 3.732 (dim 1.368).
  Spectre: ratios 9.25-10.8 (mean 9.8), dims 1.08-1.15 -> consistent with 5+2sqrt6 = 9.899 (dim 1.111),
       clearly above the polygonal value mu = 7.873.
Both published values reproduced independently. The same pipeline applies to any candidate that comes with a
hierarchy, and the PCA version (bounded spread of the 4D lift) applies to any large patch without one.

## 7. Tests of the conjectures against all known patterns (9 Nov)
Known aperiodic monotiles (single shape, possibly decorated), by class:
| tile | dim | class | inflation | in tile's own field? | window | source |
| hat family Tile(a,b), a != b | 2 | quasiperiodic | mu = phi^2, s = 3 | no (sqrt5) | hexagon with fractal parts, nu = 2+sqrt3 | Smith et al.; Baake et al. |
| Spectre Tile(1,1) | 2 | quasiperiodic | mu = 4+sqrt15, s = 8 | no (sqrt15) | fractal, nu = 5+2sqrt6 | Baake et al. |
| Taylor-Socolar (decorated / non-connected) | 2 | LIMIT-PERIODIC | 2 | rational | 2-adic internal space | Lee-Moody, arXiv 1207.6237 |
| Socolar-Taylor 3D, Schmitt-Conway-Danzer | 3 | weakly aperiodic (periodic stacking / screw) | - | - | - | |
| Chair44 (Sept 2026, decorated chair) | 3 | LIMIT-PERIODIC | 2 | rational | 2-adic | arXiv 2609.23783 |
Multi-tile comparison set: Penrose (phi, polygonal), Ammann-Beenker (1+sqrt2, polygonal), shield and Stampfli
(2+sqrt3, dodecagonal polygonal), Niizeki-Gaehler square-triangle (2+sqrt3, FRACTAL windows - Baake, Klitzing,
Schlottmann), Danzer 7-fold (cubic inflation, fractal).

Verdicts:
- H1 (quadratic unit, s integer): holds for both quasiperiodic monotiles. FAILS as stated for the class of all
  monotiles: Taylor-Socolar and Chair44 have inflation 2, not a unit. Revised H1': monotiles split into two classes,
  quasiperiodic (irrational unit inflation, Euclidean internal space) and limit-periodic (integer inflation, p-adic
  internal space). All known connected 2D monotiles are in the first class; the only known strongly aperiodic 3D
  monotile is in the second.
- H2 (inflation outside the tile's own field): holds for both quasiperiodic monotiles; trivially inapplicable to the
  limit-periodic class. Multi-tile sets all have the inflation INSIDE their field (Penrose, A-B, shield, Stampfli,
  square-triangle), so H2 does separate monotiles from multi-tile sets in every known case. Watch-out: Gummelt's
  decagon (a single-tile COVERING with overlaps) has phi inside its field, so H2 is about tilings, not coverings.
- H5 (monotile => fractal window): holds for both quasiperiodic monotiles. The converse is false (square-triangle
  and Danzer have fractal windows with several tiles), as expected.
- H4 (s, s'): hat (3,4), Spectre (8,10). New observation: all four units are the smallest totally positive units
  (norm +1) of their real quadratic fields: phi^2 in Q(sqrt5), 2+sqrt3 in Q(sqrt3), 4+sqrt15 in Q(sqrt15),
  5+2sqrt6 in Q(sqrt6). Fields: hat (5, 3), Spectre (15 = 5*3, 6 = 2*3).
Honest status: every conjecture is consistent with every known pattern, but the quasiperiodic monotile class has
only two members. The theory cannot be confirmed from the literature; it needs new members, which is what the
search is for. Concrete prediction to test: a 12-direction quasiperiodic monotile cannot have s = 4; an 8-direction
one cannot have s = 6 (silver).
